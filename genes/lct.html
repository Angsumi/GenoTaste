LCT: Dairy & Lactose Digestion (LCT Gene)

Dictates whether your body continues producing the lactase enzyme into adulthood to digest milk sugar (lactose) comfortably.

🧬 Biological Mechanism & Pathways

Lactase breaks down lactose into simple sugars in the small intestine. A regulatory region (MCM6/LCT) determines adult expression. Persistent genotypes (T/T or C/T) continue lactase production, while non-persistent genotypes (C/C) naturally down-regulate lactase, leading to lactose fermentation in the colon.

📊 Genotype Variants & Health Impact

Lactose Persistent (T/T or C/T) ~35% global population

Sustained adult lactase enzyme production; comfortable digestion of milk, yogurt, and fresh dairy.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Can include quality dairy foods as a source of calcium and protein if desired.
Lactose Non-Persistent (C/C) ~65% global population

Natural decline in adult lactase production; consumption of fresh milk causes bloating or gas.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Choose plant-based milks, fermented lactose-free dairy (aged cheddar, kefir), or use lactase enzyme drops.

🥗 Key Dietary Adjustments

  • Non-persistent (C/C) individuals can safely enjoy hard aged cheeses (parmesan, Swiss) as lactose is converted to lactic acid during aging.
  • Incorporate plant-based calcium sources (tahini, canned sardines, leafy greens).
  • Pair dairy alternatives with Vitamin D3 and K2 for calcium utilization.

⚡ High-Leverage Lifestyle Actions

  • Distinguish between milk protein allergy (casein IgE) and simple lactase enzyme insufficiency (LCT genotype).
  • Use our Personalized Diet Calculator to align your gut profile with dairy choices.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can lactose non-persistent (C/C) people ever eat dairy?

Yes! Fermented dairy products like genuine kefir, yogurt, and aged cheeses contain live bacteria that digest lactose for you, making them very well tolerated.

Is lactose non-persistence a genetic disorder?

No! Lactase non-persistence (C/C) is actually the ancestral human trait. Adult lactase persistence evolved alongside dairy farming populations.
