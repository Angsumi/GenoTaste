FUT2: Gut Microbiome & Vitamin B12 (FUT2 Gene)

Determines your gut secretor status—whether your body secretes blood group markers into the gut lining to feed beneficial probiotic bacteria and absorb Vitamin B12.

🧬 Biological Mechanism & Pathways

FUT2 produces an enzyme that secretes blood group sugar markers into body fluids and intestinal mucus. Secretors feed beneficial Bifidobacteria strains in the gut lining. Non-secretors lack these sugar markers, resulting in lower baseline beneficial gut bacteria and reduced intestinal Vitamin B12 absorption.

📊 Genotype Variants & Health Impact

Gut Secretor (FUT2 Active) ~80% of population

Abundant gut glycans; healthy Bifidobacteria colonization and standard B12 absorption.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Maintain prebiotic fiber intake to nourish native gut bacteria.
Gut Non-Secretor (FUT2 Inactive) ~20% of population

Absence of sugar markers in gut mucus; lower baseline Bifidobacteria and reduced B12 absorption efficiency.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Supplement with targeted Bifidobacterium probiotics and check Vitamin B12 blood levels.

🥗 Key Dietary Adjustments

  • Non-secretors should include fermented foods (kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi) for live beneficial bacteria.
  • Consume prebiotic fibers (galactooligosaccharides, acacia fiber) to support microbiome diversity.
  • Ensure bioavailable Vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin) intake if blood levels are low.

⚡ High-Leverage Lifestyle Actions

  • Perform a gut microbiome analysis if you experience digestive irregularity.
  • Avoid frequent unnecessary antibiotic or NSAID use that disrupts the gut mucosal lining.
  • Pair gut optimization with MTHFR methylation management for complete energy balance.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is a gut non-secretor?

A non-secretor lacks active FUT2 enzyme activity, meaning their body does not release blood group sugar markers into gut mucus. This shapes their native intestinal bacterial ecosystem.

How does FUT2 relate to B12 deficiency?

Non-secretors often have lower efficiency in intestinal B12 transport, making bioavailable B12 supplementation beneficial.
