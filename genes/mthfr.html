MTHFR: Methylenetetrahydrofolate Reductase (B-Vitamin Methylation Gene)

MTHFR converts dietary folate into its active coenzyme form (L-methylfolate), critical for cellular methylation, DNA repair, neurotransmitter synthesis, and homocysteine conversion.

🧬 Biological Mechanism & Pathways

The MTHFR enzyme catalyzes the reduction of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF). 5-MTHF donates a methyl group to convert toxic homocysteine into methionine. Common functional variants C677T and A1298C reduce enzymatic activity by 30% to 70%, leading to reduced methylation capacity and potential homocysteine accumulation if unmanaged.

📊 Genotype Variants & Health Impact

677 C/C (Wild Type) ~45% of population

100% enzymatic efficiency. Normal conversion of folate into 5-MTHF.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Maintain standard dietary folate intake from leafy greens and whole foods.
677 C/T (Heterozygous) ~40% of population

~65% enzymatic efficiency. Mild reduction in methylation processing capacity.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Prioritize natural folate sources (spinach, asparagus) over synthetic folic acid.
677 T/T (Homozygous Variant) ~10-15% of population

~30% enzymatic efficiency. Significant reduction in folate conversion and methylation efficiency.

GenoTaste Recommendation: Incorporate active bioavailable L-methylfolate (5-MTHF) and methylcobalamin (B12) under consulting guidance.

🥗 Key Dietary Adjustments

  • Eat abundant dark leafy greens (spinach, romaine, avocado, lentils) rich in natural folates.
  • Avoid foods fortified with synthetic folic acid, as unmetabolized folic acid can block folate receptors in MTHFR variant carriers.
  • Ensure adequate intake of riboflavin (Vitamin B2), which acts as an essential cofactor for MTHFR enzymatic stability.

⚡ High-Leverage Lifestyle Actions

  • Test plasma homocysteine levels annually as part of routine preventive wellness.
  • Support phase II liver detoxification with choline and betaine (trimethylglycine).
  • Consult GenoTaste specialists to evaluate optimal bioavailable B-complex balances.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Folic Acid and Methylfolate?

Folic acid is a synthetic oxidized form of folate that must undergo multiple enzymatic conversions (including MTHFR) to become active. L-Methylfolate (5-MTHF) is already bioactive and bypasses MTHFR bottlenecks.

Does having an MTHFR mutation mean I have a disease?

No. MTHFR variants are common biological trait variations, not diseases. They simply highlight a specific nutritional requirement for bioactive folates and B-vitamins.
